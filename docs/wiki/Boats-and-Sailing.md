# Boats & Sailing

There's a rowboat tied at the pier. You can build more with the **Rowboat** blueprint.

- **Board:** walk up to the boat and press F.
- **Row:** W / S forward and back, A / D turn. Hold Shift to pull hard (uses stamina).
- **Step off:** press F. Near land you step ashore. Out on open water you go **over the side
  and swim**, so you can dive for things or fish from the water. Swim back and press F to climb in.
- Storms bring big swell. Keep the bow into the waves.
- Boats drift with the current while nobody's rowing, so tie up at a pier.

## The outrigger skiff
For longer voyages, build an **Outrigger Skiff** at a workbench (24 planks, 8 driftwood,
10 kelp fronds, 4 sea wool). It comes as a kit: stand at the water's edge with the kit in
hand, find water at least waist deep, and press F to launch it.

- **Sail:** W raises the sail and S lets it down; A / D steer. The skiff goes where the
  **wind** lets it. It is fastest with the wind on its side (a beam reach), a little slower
  running straight downwind, and it can't sail into the wind at all. The prompt shows your
  point of sail.
- **Paddle:** hold Shift to paddle when the wind is against you or you're nosing into a pier.
  It's slow, but it always works.
- It seats two, and a float on booms keeps it steady in a swell.

## The Tide-Barge
A flat-bottomed workboat for hauling. Build a **Tide-Barge kit** at a workbench and launch it
like the skiff.

- **Seats four**, rowed with long sweep oars: slow to get going and slow to turn, but steady.
- **Cargo hold (27 slots):** stand beside the barge, hold a stack, and press **Shift+F** to
  stow it. Shift+F with an empty hand takes the first stack back out. Anyone in your crew can
  use the hold, and it is saved with the boat.
- It **floats over the mud flats**, so you can bring stone and timber right up the beach at
  low tide where other boats would ground.

## The Coastal Sloop
A single-mast day-sailer with a small cabin. Build a **Coastal Sloop kit** at a workbench.

- **Seats three.** Sails like the skiff (W raises the sail, S lowers it, Shift to scull), but
  its gaff sail **points higher into the wind** and it is quicker on every point of sail.
- The best boat for reaching the far islands and the reaver outposts.

## Mooring bollards
Build a **mooring bollard** (4 driftwood, 1 iron ingot) on a pier or the shore. An empty boat
left beside one stays put instead of drifting off on the current.

Every boat you launch is saved with the world (along with what's in a barge's hold), so
it's still there when you come back.

## The lighthouse
A lighthouse stands on the coast beside your hut. From dusk to dawn its beams sweep the sea,
so you can always find your way home after a night sail.

## Shipwrecks
Broken trading cutters lie driven ashore on beaches around the archipelago, heeled over on
the tideline with their ribs showing and the mast snapped on the sand. The tide washes
through them. Each has a **Wreck Locker** inside: planks, a purse of brass guilders, some
salvage, and a **rare recipe scroll** (see [Crafting & Recipes](Crafting-and-Recipes.md)).
There's never a wreck right on your doorstep; sail out and look along the beaches.

Some wrecks hide a **castaway** pinned under the timbers. You can only reach them at low
tide. Talk to them (F) to pull them free, and they'll make their own way to your village.
See [Village & Drifters](Village-and-Drifters.md).

## The islands
Brine's Hollow is your home island, about 700 m across. The archipelago around it is about
2.6 km wide. It holds eight larger islands, each with its own character (a flat meadow, a
sandy atoll, rolling hills, rocky crags and a cliff-sided mesa, and more of each), plus a
scatter of small islets and sea stacks, with open sea between them. Hills and cliffs rise up
to about 13 m above the sea. Every island has a name that everyone in the world shares.

Worlds made before the bigger archipelago keep their original, smaller layout (about 1 km
across, with five larger islands), so nothing you've built moves. They show as "classic size"
in the world list. Start a new world to get the big one.

The first time you set foot on an island, its name comes up with **"New island
discovered"** and how many you've charted so far. Discovered islands are named on your sea
chart (equip a chart in the map slot). Each character remembers their own discoveries in that
world.

Look for sea caves in the island cliffs, caverns under the high ground and old brass mines
in the hillsides (see [Caves & Mining](Caves-and-Mining.md)). Some outer islands are held by
Mud-Claws (see [Creatures & Wildlife](Creatures-and-Wildlife.md)).

Out past the shallows you'll find deep-sea fish, seals and puffins on the skerries, and the
occasional whale passing by (see [Creatures & Wildlife](Creatures-and-Wildlife.md)).
